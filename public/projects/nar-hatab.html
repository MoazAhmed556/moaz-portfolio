Our story

Nar / Hatab is built around a single hearth of oak and olive wood — hatab, split and stacked by the door every morning. It is lit at noon and allowed to settle for hours, so that by evening there are three kinds of heat to cook with: flame for bread, hot coals for skewers, and deep embers for anything that needs time.

The menu follows the Levantine table — mezze first, then whatever comes off the fire, then something sweet with mint tea. Nothing is plated for one person only. You'll pass bowls, tear bread and argue about the last piece of kibbeh, which is exactly the point.

“We light the hearth at noon and wait. Everything good here depends on that patience.”

— The kitchen (sample brand voice)

i.One fire

Every station draws its heat from the same hearth. When the coals are ready, the grill opens; when the embers are deep, the lamb goes in.

ii.Made to share

Plates arrive as they're ready, in the middle of the table. Order a few mezze, one thing from the fire, and keep going.

iii.The season decides

Figs in late summer, blood oranges in winter, wild herbs in spring. The core menu stays; the details move with the market.

    About this demo

    Nar / Hatab is a fictional restaurant. Every interaction on this page works, but anything that would need a restaurant's own systems is simulated and labelled.

    Works in this page

    • Ordering method, table selection by code or floor plan
    • Menu filters, dish options, extras and validation
    • Cart, quantities, variants, totals, service charge and demo delivery fee
    • Checkout validation and a confirmed demo order
    • Feedback form with validation (kept in page memory only)

    Simulated

    • Table verification — codes are only checked against this page's floor plan
    • The kitchen timeline (a timed state machine, sped up)
    • Delivery fee and times — no address check, distance or zone lookup
    • Address, phone numbers and opening hours
    • Sample guest notes (not real reviews)

    Needs a backend

    • Table registry and signed QR codes
    • Order submission, kitchen display and live status updates
    • Payments, receipts and refunds
    • Delivery zones, address validation and rider tracking
    • Storing feedback and opening hours

    Needs HTTPS or localhost

    • Camera QR scanning: browsers only allow the camera on secure pages, and only after you allow it. Opened as a local file, the scanner explains this and offers code entry or the floor plan instead.

    External resources

    • Fonts from Google Fonts (Fraunces, Manrope, Reem Kufi) — falls back to system fonts.
    • Photos from images.unsplash.com — need internet. Tiny blurred previews are embedded so the layout and colour still show offline.
    • jsQR 1.4.0 from cdn.jsdelivr.net — loaded only if you start scanning in a browser without the built-in BarcodeDetector.
